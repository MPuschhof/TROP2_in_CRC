# TROP2 in CRC
TROP2 targeting reveals therapy-driven cell state transitions in human colorectal cancer 

Metastatic colorectal cancer (CRC) is a leading cause of cancer-related death driven by inefficient therapeutic options and highly plastic cancer cells. Here, we uncover the transmembrane glycoprotein trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2) as a marker for poor prognosis CRC. TROP2 labels highly plastic, WNT-signaling-low, fetal-like and high-relapse cell states linked to metastatic progression and therapy resistance. Functional analysis identifies that TROP2+ cells are context-dependent cancer stem cells (CSCs) and metastasis-initiating cells (MICs). Importantly, pharmacological targeting of poor prognosis associated TROP2-expressing cells by applying the antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) Sacituzumab Govitecan (SG) was accompanied by a cell state transition towards WNT-high LGR5+ intestinal stem cell (ISC) states. Contrary, chemotherapy induces TROP2 expression levels. Our findings therefore define a clinically relevant targeting strategy in which combination of chemotherapy with SG enhances antitumor efficacy and circumvents adaptive therapy resistance in advanced CRC.
